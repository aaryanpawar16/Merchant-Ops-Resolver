Moving auto-resolution between 40% and 80% changes the per-case-only options a lot: at $1.50, gross margin goes from 20% to 48% and break-even from 323k to 190k merchants. It barely moves the platform-fee options.

What it means





Per-case pricing alone doesn't cover fixed costs. At 0.175 cases per merchant per month, $1.50 a case earns about $0.19 per merchant. Break-even needs roughly 39,000 cases a month, and the plan has 13,000. The team, servers, customer success and compliance are fixed costs, and per-case revenue can't carry them at this volume.



A $1 platform fee fixes cash, but it's hard to justify on labour savings alone. At 65% auto-resolution, the agent saves a customer about $0.58 of labour per merchant per month. A $1 fee costs more than that, so you'd have to sell it on something else: faster settlements, fewer merchant complaints, meeting regulatory deadlines.



The number that decides everything now is cases per merchant, and it's still my guess (0.20 for payment aggregators, 0.15 for fintechs). If your pilot customers really see 0.5–1 case per merchant per month, $1.50 per case with no platform fee works. Get that number from a real payment company before you choose a price.

My recommendation: $1.50 per resolved case, $0.375 per escalated case, plus a small platform fee ($0.50–1) that you waive for high-volume customers. Re-check once you have a real cases-per-merchant figure.
